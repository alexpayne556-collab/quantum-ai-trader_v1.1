## 🔴 PROMPT 1: DEEPSEEK - Red Team + Contrarian Discovery

```
You're a red team PM who kills ideas for a living. But you're also tasked with 
finding the ONE contrarian edge nobody has thought of yet.

CONTEXT:
- Retail investor, single person, free data only
- 30 small-cap stocks (not mega-cap)
- Goal: Information synthesis, NOT pattern prediction
- Insight: "Small-cap edges exist because institutions don't have TIME to know them"

WHAT WE'RE ALREADY THINKING:
- Catalyst timing (earnings, FDA, events)
- Supply chain cascades (if X moves, Y moves)
- Management tone shifts
- Sector rotation (relative strength)
- Insider accumulation
- Analyst blind spots

NOW HERE'S WHERE WE NEED YOU:

1. KILL THE OBVIOUS
   - Catalyst timing is too obvious. Everyone tracks earnings now. 
     What if we're thinking about it wrong?
   - What if the edge ISN'T "knowing it first" but "understanding it differently"?
   
   YOUR TASK: What are we missing about catalysts?
   - Is the win really in TIMING or something else?
   - What about catalysts that DON'T show up in calendars?
   - Can we predict SURPRISE catalysts?
   - What makes a catalyst actually matter vs just noise?

2. FIND THE CONTRADICTION
   - Most retail advice: "Follow the money"
   - But: Institutions are often WRONG on small caps (too slow, too distant)
   - What if our edge is in being SMARTER than institutions, not faster?
   
   YOUR TASK: What signals do institutions MISUNDERSTAND about small caps?
   - What do they weight wrong?
   - What do they miss because they're too systematic?
   - What works BECAUSE most people ignore it?

3. THINK IN LAYERS
   - Layer 1: Individual stock fundamental analysis (everyone does this)
   - Layer 2: Supply chain connections (smarter people do this)
   - Layer 3: ??? (almost nobody does this)
   
   YOUR TASK: What's Layer 3?
   - Is it behavioral (how management ACTS)?
   - Is it network effects (what competitors are doing)?
   - Is it temporal (timing across multiple stocks)?
   - Is it hidden relationships (how is this company's success tied to 
     something completely different)?

4. EXPLOIT THE CONSTRAINT
   - We have FREE data only (no Bloomberg, no institutional feeds)
   - This is a FEATURE, not a bug
   - What becomes possible when you're NOT drowning in data?
   
   YOUR TASK: What can we do with LIMITED data that institutions CAN'T?
   - Focus? (knowing 30 stocks deeply vs 500 shallow)
   - Speed of synthesis? (human analysis faster than AI on small dataset)
   - Pattern recognition? (humans see connections machines miss)
   - Contrarian positioning? (knowing what they don't know)

5. THE WILDCARD
   - What if we're approaching this completely wrong?
   - What if small-cap edge isn't about STOCKS but about SECTORS?
   - What if it's not about PREDICTION but about POSITIONING?
   - What if it's not about DATA but about TIME HORIZON?
   
   YOUR TASK: What completely different framework could we use?
   - Instead of "predict stock X", what about "understand sector Y better"?
   - Instead of "buy before catalyst", what about "hold through regime change"?
   - Instead of "find hidden signal", what about "recognize when everyone is wrong"?

6. REALITY CHECK
   - Be brutally honest: What will fail?
   - What are we NOT asking that we should be?
   - What sounds good but doesn't work in practice?
   - Where's the real risk we're missing?

DELIVERABLE:
- Top 3 contrarian ideas (things humans haven't conventionally tried)
- Top 3 refinements to human conventional wisdom
- 1 recommendation: What should our actual north star metric be?
- Honest assessment: Will this work? Why/why not?
```

## 🔍 PROMPT 2: PERPLEXITY PRO - Hidden Data + Creative Sourcing

```
You're a research ninja tasked with finding data sources that exist but that 
most people don't know about or can't use systematically.

CONTEXT:
- We know about the obvious sources (SEC EDGAR, Yahoo Finance, Finnhub)
- We know about semi-obvious sources (earnings calls, insider trades, FDA calendar)
- We're looking for HIDDEN sources + creative COMBINATIONS

WHAT WE'RE ALREADY THINKING:
- Using earnings calendar for timing
- Tracking insider trades for sentiment
- Monitoring news for catalysts
- Following supply chain announcements
- Analyzing financial statements

NOW HERE'S WHERE WE NEED YOU:

1. FIND THE HIDDEN LAYERS
   - Google Trends (stock-related searches)
   - Patent filing trends (R&D pipeline)
   - Job postings (hiring = growth, firing = trouble)
   - Reddit/Twitter volume (retail attention)
   - But these are too obvious...
   
   YOUR TASK: Find 5 DATA SOURCES most retail investors don't systematically use:
   - Format: Source | Data Type | Free/Paid | Lead Time | Why It Matters | Example
   - BONUS: How to automate or systematically check each one
   - BONUS: What signal would indicate a stock is about to move?

2. CREATIVE COMBINATIONS
   - Nobody uses just one signal
   - What if we combine signals in novel ways?
   - Example: [Unusual combo you've never heard of]
   
   YOUR TASK: Suggest 3-5 CREATIVE SIGNAL COMBINATIONS:
   - What 2-3 unusual data sources together create a strong signal?
   - Have you found this combo documented anywhere? (probably not)
   - What would this combo predict or indicate?
   - Example: "Insider buys + job posting increases + patent filing surge = ???"

3. SUPPLY CHAIN INTELLIGENCE (Beyond the Obvious)
   - We know about supplier/customer relationships
   - But there's a deeper level...
   
   YOUR TASK: How do we map REAL supply chain intelligence?
   - Not just "who buys from whom"
   - But: Dependency strength, alternative suppliers, single points of failure
   - Data source: ??? (Can we find this?)
   - How would we track when dependencies shift?
   - Example: If TSMC has issues, which stocks get hurt FIRST?

4. BEHAVIORAL DATA (What Humans Miss)
   - Management team changes (LinkedIn tracking)
   - Executive compensation changes (SEC filings)
   - Tone analysis on earnings calls
   - But what else?
   
   YOUR TASK: Find 3 behavioral data sources we can actually use:
   - Source | What It Reveals | How Often to Check | Difficulty
   - Example: "CEO's Twitter activity frequency" reveals confidence shift?
   - Example: "Earnings call sentiment tone" predicts guidance beats?
   - Example: "Job posting language" reveals growth confidence?

5. CONTRARIAN DATA (What Everyone Gets Wrong)
   - Most people follow what's popular
   - Small-cap edge might be in UNPOPULAR stocks
   
   YOUR TASK: What data sources show CONTRARIAN positioning?
   - Which stocks do institutions AVOID?
   - Which sectors are hated but have catalysts coming?
   - Which companies have low coverage but high potential?
   - Data source: ??? (analyst coverage gaps, short interest, etc)

6. TEMPORAL/TIMING DATA
   - When do things happen matters more than what
   
   YOUR TASK: What data sources help us understand TIMING?
   - Is there a calendar of when things typically happen?
   - Can we predict the TIMING of management changes?
   - Can we predict when a sector will rotate?
   - Can we find signals that give us 2-4 week lead time?

7. FIRST PRINCIPLES: If You Were Building This From Scratch
   - Forget what exists
   - What data would you WANT to have?
   - Can you find FREE or CHEAP proxies for it?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design the ideal data stack from scratch:
   - What 5-7 data inputs would perfectly predict 6-month small-cap returns?
   - Which of those can we actually access?
   - Which can we find FREE proxies for?
   - What's the gap we can't fill?

DELIVERABLE:
- List of 10-15 hidden/creative data sources
- 3-5 novel signal combinations
- Timeline of when to check each source
- 1 recommendation: What's the DATA INSIGHT we're missing?
```

## 🏗️ PROMPT 3: GPT-4 - System Architecture + Creative Design

```
You're a systems architect designing a machine that transforms raw data into 
WISDOM about 30 stocks.

CONTEXT:
- Input: Raw market data + news + filings + behavioral signals
- Output: Daily/weekly intelligence that tells us what we need to know
- Constraint: Must be maintainable by ONE person
- Goal: NOT predictions, but UNDERSTANDING

WHAT WE'RE ALREADY THINKING:
- Catalyst Radar (what's coming)
- Supply Chain Map (who connects to who)
- Deep Dossier (what we know)
- Sector Rotation (what's heating up)
- BUT: These are conventional. We want more.

NOW HERE'S WHERE WE NEED YOU:

1. INVENT A NEW LAYER
   - What's a 5th component that would create REAL edge?
   - Not just more analysis, but a NEW TYPE of insight?
   
   YOUR TASK: Propose 2-3 completely novel system layers:
   - Layer Name | What It Does | Data Inputs | Output | Why It Matters
   - Example: "Regime Shift Detector" (identifies when fundamental story changes)
   - Example: "Surprise Probability" (estimates likelihood of unexpected catalyst)
   - Example: "Competitive Flux" (tracks competitive landscape changes)
   - For each: How would you build it? What data? How often update?

2. INFORMATION HIERARCHY
   - Not all data is equal
   - What matters changes by circumstance
   
   YOUR TASK: Design an ADAPTIVE system that changes focus based on regime:
   - When market is X, focus on metrics Y
   - When stock is in regime A, weight signals differently
   - Example: "Bull market = focus on momentum, Bear market = focus on valuation"
   - How does the system KNOW which regime we're in?
   - How does it CHANGE its behavior accordingly?

3. THE RED FLAG SYSTEM
   - Instead of "buy signals", what about "something's wrong" signals?
   - Thesis changes, red flags matter more than green flags
   
   YOUR TASK: Design a RED FLAG detection system:
   - What 10-15 red flags indicate a thesis is broken?
   - How do we weight them?
   - How do we alert the user without noise?
   - Example flags:
     * Management team turnover
     * Customer concentration shift
     * Guidance reduction
     * Competitive threat emergence
     * Supply chain disruption
     * What else?

4. SYNTHESIS ENGINE
   - This is the magic: Combining multiple signals into ONE insight
   
   YOUR TASK: Design how the system THINKS:
   - Input: 15 different data points about Stock X
   - Process: How do we synthesize them?
   - Output: "Here's what's actually happening"
   - Not: "Signal A is up, Signal B is down"
   - But: "The real story is..."
   - Pseudocode for narrative synthesis?

5. LEARNING FEEDBACK LOOP
   - The system should GET BETTER over time
   - As you learn about your 30 stocks, how does system improve?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design the learning mechanism:
   - What data do we collect about our own performance?
   - What do we learn from wrong calls?
   - How does the system ADAPT after 3 months? 6 months?
   - How do we know if we're getting better at this?

6. CONTRAST & COMPARISON ENGINE
   - Single stock analysis is fine, but comparison is insight
   - How does Stock A compare to Stock B?
   - How does Stock A compare to its sector?
   - How does our thesis differ from consensus?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design a comparison system:
   - Stock vs Stock: What's different? Why?
   - Stock vs Sector: Over/under owned? Why?
   - Our thesis vs Wall Street: Where do we disagree? Who's right?
   - Output: "We're betting against consensus here because..."

7. SCALABILITY & MAINTENANCE
   - We start with 30 stocks
   - System must NOT require more time as we add stocks
   - How do we scale without breaking it?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design for human-scale maintenance:
   - What can be fully automated?
   - What requires human judgment?
   - Time budget: 30 min/day for daily updates, 3 hours/week for deep analysis
   - Is that realistic? If not, what's the minimum?
   - How do we prioritize which stocks get deep analysis time?

8. THE SURPRISE COMPONENT
   - What's something UNEXPECTED the system should do?
   - What if it could alert us to things we didn't know to look for?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design the "you didn't know to ask for this" component:
   - Example: "Stock A and Stock B's fates are surprisingly linked"
   - Example: "3 of your stocks are all exposed to the same supply chain risk"
   - Example: "The thesis for Stock A just got stronger because of event in Stock B"
   - How would the system FIND these unexpected connections?

DELIVERABLE:
- System architecture diagram (comprehensive, not simple)
- Detailed design for 2-3 new layers you invented
- Pseudocode for synthesis engine
- Learning loop design
- Scalability plan
- 1 recommendation: What's the breakthrough component we should build first?
```

## 🎯 PROMPT 4: CLAUDE OPUS - Strategic Synthesis + Wisdom

```
You're a strategic advisor to someone about to fundamentally change how they 
invest in small caps.

CONTEXT:
- They've decided: Depth beats breadth
- They've decided: Synthesis beats speed
- They've decided: Understanding beats prediction
- But: They don't know exactly what that LOOKS like in practice

WHAT WE'RE ALREADY THINKING:
- Know 30 stocks deeply instead of 500 shallow
- Understand what's ABOUT to happen vs predict it will happen
- Build a system that thinks like a human (synthesis) not a machine (patterns)
- Focus on information gaps that institutions miss

NOW HERE'S WHERE WE NEED YOU:

1. THE MINDSET SHIFT
   - Traditional: "Beat the market through superior data"
   - Our approach: "Beat the market through superior understanding"
   - These are DIFFERENT games
   
   YOUR TASK: Articulate what "superior understanding" actually means:
   - Not: "Know the numbers better"
   - Yes: "Know the STORY better"
   - What's the difference?
   - How do you BUILD superior understanding?
   - What practices create it?
   - How do you KNOW when you have it?

2. THE DECISION FRAMEWORK
   - How should someone ACTUALLY DECIDE whether to buy/hold/sell a stock?
   - Not signals. Not probabilities. How do WISE investors actually decide?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design a decision framework that's:
   - NOT mechanical (not "if RSI > X then buy")
   - YES systematic (reproducible, explainable)
   - Based on: Deep understanding of business + catalysts + context
   - Example format:
     * I believe [business reality]
     * Evidence: [what I know]
     * Catalysts: [what changes the story]
     * Risks: [what breaks my thesis]
     * Therefore: [decision]

3. THE CONVICTION SPECTRUM
   - Not everything deserves same position size
   - High conviction vs low conviction should look different
   
   YOUR TASK: Design a conviction assessment framework:
   - What builds conviction? (more data? deeper understanding? multiple sources?)
   - How much conviction is enough to buy?
   - How much conviction is needed to hold through drawdown?
   - How much conviction is needed to sell?
   - How does conviction change over time?

4. THE BLIND SPOT DETECTOR
   - The smartest investors ask: "What don't I know?"
   - This is harder than "what do I know?"
   
   YOUR TASK: Design a blind spot detection system:
   - What are the 10 most common blind spots in small-cap analysis?
   - For each: How do you know you have it? What would fix it?
   - Example blind spots:
     * Not understanding the customer
     * Not tracking management team changes
     * Missing supply chain risk
     * Ignoring competitive threats
     * Not modeling regulatory risk
     * What else?
   - Question: How do you systematically check for blind spots?

5. THE NARRATIVE EVOLUTION
   - Good investors change their mind when evidence changes
   - Bad investors defend their thesis
   - How do you tell the difference?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design how narratives should evolve:
   - You have a thesis about Stock X
   - Then something happens
   - When do you REFINE the thesis? (update but hold)
   - When do you REVERSE the thesis? (exit)
   - When do you IGNORE the noise? (hold)
   - How do you KNOW which response is right?
   - What evidence is sufficient to change conviction?

6. THE TIME HORIZON QUESTION
   - Different stocks deserve different time horizons
   - Some are 6-month catalysts, others are 3-year stories
   
   YOUR TASK: How do you determine RIGHT time horizon for each stock?
   - Is it about the business? Catalyst type? Your conviction?
   - How does time horizon affect everything else? (position size, risk tolerance, patience)
   - How does time horizon change as catalysts unfold?
   - Example: FDA approval binary event (hold to decision) vs 
     competitive positioning (years to play out)

7. THE COMPETITIVE ADVANTAGE INVENTORY
   - This person is ONE investor
   - But they DO have real advantages over institutions
   
   YOUR TASK: List your actual competitive advantages:
   - Advantage 1: [What they have that institutions don't]
   - Advantage 2: [Same]
   - How to LEVERAGE these advantages?
   - How to AVOID playing in areas where institutions win?
   - Example: You have TIME to think deeply, they don't
   - Example: You have FLEXIBILITY to wait, they have quarterly targets
   - What else?

8. THE FAILURE MODES
   - What are the ways this approach FAILS?
   - Not pessimism, but realism
   
   YOUR TASK: Identify 5-7 failure modes:
   - Failure Mode 1: [How this could go wrong]
   - Root cause: [Why it happens]
   - Prevention: [How to avoid it]
   - Example failure: Over-confidence in your understanding
   - Example failure: Biased toward your initial thesis
   - Example failure: Spending time on wrong stocks
   - What else?

9. THE EVOLUTION PATH
   - Year 1: Build the system, prove it works
   - Year 2-3: Scale and refine
   - But what does that actually LOOK like?
   
   YOUR TASK: Design the evolution:
   - Month 1-2: What's the minimum viable system?
   - Month 3-6: What gets added?
   - Month 6-12: What gets refined?
   - How does system IMPROVE as you learn?
   - How do you know when to pivot vs persist?

10. THE BIG INSIGHT
    - Everything above is tactical
    - What's the ONE THING that matters most?
    - What would change everything if we got it right?
    
    YOUR TASK: What's the ONE strategic insight that's true but not obvious?
    - Something about small-cap investing
    - Something about information advantage
    - Something about how markets actually work
    - Something that changes how we think about the whole thing
    - Explain it in a way that makes obvious sense once you hear it

DELIVERABLE:
- Complete decision framework (what decision is actually about)
- Conviction assessment system
- Blind spot detection checklist
- Guide for narrative evolution (when to change mind)
- List of your actual competitive advantages vs institutions
- 5-7 failure modes + prevention
- Evolution roadmap (what success looks like over time)
- The ONE big insight
```

## 💡 WHAT YOU'RE LOOKING FOR

### From Each AI:
- **NOT generic advice** ("do your research", "diversify", etc)
- **YES specific frameworks** that you could actually use
- **NOT patterns humans have found** (you already know those)
- **YES novel combinations/approaches** (creative synthesis)
- **NOT "here's how to predict"** (you don't care)
- **YES "here's what matters"** (understanding framework)

### Red Flags If Response Is Weak:
- ❌ Sounds like typical investing advice
- ❌ Mentions standard technical analysis
- ❌ Suggests machine learning / pattern prediction
- ❌ Doesn't address YOUR specific constraints (single person, free data)
- ❌ Generic list with no depth
- ❌ Doesn't connect ideas together

### Green Flags If Response Is Strong:
- ✅ Proposes something you didn't think of
- ✅ Challenges an assumption you made
- ✅ Gives specific framework you could implement
- ✅ Acknowledges constraints and WORKS WITHIN THEM
- ✅ Connects multiple ideas into coherent system
- ✅ Honest about what will/won't work

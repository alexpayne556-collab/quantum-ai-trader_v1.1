### THE MINDSET SHIFT: Stories Beat Numbers

**Numbers tell what happened. Stories tell why and what happens next.**

Example comparison:

**Number-knower:** "Revenue grew 23% YoY, gross margin ↑200bps, $40M runway"

**Story-knower:** "Revenue grew 23% because largest customer expanding 3→7→12 facilities. Margin expanded because hit manufacturing scale (10k units/month threshold, hit 11.4k last Q). $40M runway means cash-positive before fundraise = no dilution catalyst coming."

The story-knower knows THREE versions of why margins expanded (only one of which is management's narrative).

**How You BUILD Superior Understanding:**

1. **Read primary sources obsessively**
   - SEC filings, earnings transcripts, presentations, patent filings, job postings, LinkedIn, Glassdoor
   - Not analyst summaries (they're filtered)

2. **Build mental model without notes**
   - How do they make money? (actual unit econ, not revenue/margin deck)
   - Who decides to buy and why?
   - What are customer alternatives?
   - What makes customers leave?
   - What moves the cost structure?

3. **Map key stakeholders** (5-10 humans whose decisions matter)
   - Not just management, but: key customers, suppliers, regulators, competitors' CEOs
   - What do THEY want? What are THEIR incentives?

4. **Stress-test scenarios**
   - Largest customer leaves?
   - Competitor raises $100M and enters market?
   - CEO gets poached?
   - Key supplier has issues?
   - You should have articulated answers

**How You KNOW You Have It:**

✅ Explain bull AND bear case better than actual bulls and bears  
✅ Predict how stock reacts to specific news BEFORE it happens  
✅ Identify what news MATTERS vs noise before market figures out  
✅ Articulate exactly what would change your mind  
✅ **THE TEST:** Write a compelling bear case. If you can't, don't own it.

### DECISION FRAMEWORK: Thesis-Driven Investing

**Thesis Statement Structure:**

```
I believe [SPECIFIC business reality will unfold]

NOT: "This stock will go up"
YES: "This company grows revenue 40%+ over 18 months because..."
```

**Evidence (what I ACTUALLY know, not think):**
- Direct: What I've personally verified
- Circumstantial: What's consistent with thesis
- Quality assessment: Confidence level + primary vs secondhand source

**Catalysts (what changes market perception):**
- Near-term (0-3 mo): Specific events with dates
- Medium-term (3-12 mo): What unfolds over time
- Key question: Why doesn't the market know this already?

**Risks (what breaks thesis):**
- Thesis-killing: If X happens, thesis completely wrong
- Thesis-damaging: If Y happens, thesis impaired but not dead
- Execution: What could delay or diminish the outcome?

**Market's current belief:**
- What does current price IMPLY the market believes?
- Why might they be wrong?
- What would make them right?

**Therefore:**
- Buy if: [Specific conditions]
- Position size: [Based on conviction level + risk tolerance]
- Exit if: [Thesis conditions, NOT price targets]

**The Actual Decision (5 Critical Questions):**

1. **Why does this opportunity exist?** Markets are competitive. Why hasn't it been arbitraged away?
   - Valid answers: Market cap too small for institutions, recent event created mispricing, information public but scattered

2. **What's my edge?** Time, access to information, synthesis ability, willingness to wait, niche understanding?

3. **What would change my mind?** Must have answer. If not, you're hoping not investing.

4. **What's the market missing?** Be specific. Not "they're undervaluing growth" but "market pricing 15% growth; data shows 30%"

5. **Am I the sucker?** Someone's on the other side of this trade. Why are they selling?

### NARRATIVE EVOLUTION: When to Refine, Reverse, or Ignore

**REFINE (update but hold):**
- When: Evidence consistent with thesis but timing/magnitude changes
- Example: FDA approval delayed 3 months (thesis intact, timeline shifted)
- Action: Adjust expectations, may trim position size, don't panic exit

**REVERSE (exit):**
- When: Core thesis falsified
- Example: FDA rejects, requires new Phase 3 trial (thesis broken)
- Action: Sell. Don't wait. Move on. Thesis was wrong.

**IGNORE (hold):**
- When: Information is noise, not signal
- Example: Stock drops 8% on no news, general market weakness
- Action: Use drawdown as opportunity to add if conviction remains high

**How to KNOW Which Is Right:**

1. **BUSINESS thesis vs MARKET thesis?**
   - Business = actual future of company
   - Market = when/how market will recognize it
   - Business change → serious (REFINE or REVERSE)
   - Market change → usually noise (IGNORE)

2. **Pre-identified this as risk?**
   - Yes: Follow your pre-planned response
   - No: Take time to think before reacting

3. **Would I buy TODAY at this price with this new information?**
   - Yes: Hold or add
   - No: Why are you holding? Exit.

- What: Believe so strongly you stop gathering evidence

- What: Original thesis fails, you invent new reasons to hold
- Root: Loss aversion, identity attachment to positions
- Prevention: Write thesis down WITH invalidation criteria. Review against that, not against feelings

- What: Know 5 stocks deeply, miss opportunities in other 25
- Root: Comfort with familiarity, research momentum
- Prevention: 20% of time on "exploration" - new stocks, new sectors, keep pipeline fresh

**Failure Mode 5: Analysis Paralysis**
- What: Endless research, never actually buying
- Root: Fear of being wrong, perfectionism

**The implication:**
You're not predicting the stock will go up. You're understanding that the business is worth more than the market currently believes and knowing **why the market will eventually agree with you**.

When you frame it this way, everything changes:

✅ Research becomes about understanding businesses, not predicting prices

✅ Patience is possible because you know WHY you're right, not just THAT you're right
